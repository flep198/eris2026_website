1. Inspecting the Data

Before imaging any spectral-line dataset it is important to understand the structure of the observations. The first step is therefore to inspect the measurement set and determine:

  • The science target.
  • The number of spectral windows.
  • The channel structure.
  • The observing frequency.
  • The antenna configuration.

This information will be useful later when choosing imaging parameters. 

# In CASA
listobs(
    vis='twhya_selfcal.ms',
)

Take a few minutes to inspect the output. In particular identify:

  • The field corresponding to TW Hya.
  • The spectral window containing the N₂H⁺ transition.
  • The central observing frequency.
  • The total number of channels.

These quantities will be needed throughout the reduction process.

2. Determine the Line Channels

Before producing a spectral cube we must determine which channels contain spectral-line emission. We also need to identify channels that are free of line emission because these will later be used to fit and subtract the continuum.

A convenient way to do this is to average the visibilities and plot amplitude as a function of channel number. 

# In CASA
plotms(
    vis='twhya_selfcal.ms',
    xaxis='channel',
    yaxis='amp',
    field='5',
    avgspw=False,
    avgtime='1e9',
    avgscan=True,
    avgbaseline=True,
    showgui=True,
)
<"_spectrum.png" alt="TW Hya channel spectrum" />

The spectral line appears as a deviation from the continuum level. Identify the channel range occupied by the line emission and determine which channels appear free of line emission on either side of the feature.

These line-free channels will be used to construct the continuum model in the next section.

3. Continuum Subtraction

The measured visibilities contain contributions from both continuum emission and spectral-line emission.

For many scientific applications we are interested only in the spectral line itself. We therefore fit a continuum model using the line-free channels identified above and subtract this model directly from the visibilities.

Performing the subtraction in visibility space is generally preferred because it avoids imaging artefacts and provides a cleaner isolation of the spectral-line signal. 

# In CASA
os.system('rm -rf twhya_selfcal.ms.contsub')
uvcontsub(
 vis='twhya_selfcal.ms',
 field='5',
 fitspec='0:0~239;281~383',
 fitorder=0,
 outputvis='twhya_selfcal.ms.contsub',
)
# In CASA
plotms(
 vis='twhya_selfcal.ms.contsub',
 xaxis='channel',
 yaxis='amp',
 field='5',
 avgspw=False,
 avgtime='1e9',
 avgscan=True,
 avgbaseline=True,
 showgui=True,
)

The continuum-subtracted data should now have an average amplitude close to zero in the line-free channels.

4. Imaging the Spectral Line: Dirty Cube

Before performing any deconvolution it is useful to examine the dirty cube.

A dirty cube is produced by Fourier transforming the visibilities without applying any CLEAN iterations. This allows us to:

  • Estimate the image noise.
  • Inspect the quality of the data.
  • Determine which channels contain emission.
  • Decide on an appropriate cleaning strategy.

The dirty cube therefore serves as an important diagnostic before investing time in a full deconvolution.

Linux

restfreq='372.67249GHz'

tclean(
 vis='twhya_selfcal.ms.contsub',
 imagename='twhya_dirty',
 field='5',
 spw='0',
 specmode='cube',
 perchanweightdensity=True,
 nchan=15,
 start='0.0km/s',
 width='0.5km/s',
 outframe='LSRK',
 restfreq=restfreq,
 deconvolver='hogbom',
 gridder='standard',
 imsize=[250,250],
 cell='0.1arcsec',
 weighting='briggsbwtaper',
 robust=0.5,
 restoringbeam='common',
 interactive=True,
 niter=0,
 pbcor=True)

# In CASA
imstat('twhya_dirty.image', chans='0~4')

The imstat task calculates basic statistical properties of an image such as the mean, maximum intensity and RMS noise.

Measure the RMS noise in several channels that appear free of emission. The values should be broadly consistent from channel to channel.

Inspecting the Cube in CARTA

Open the dirty cube in CARTA and step through the channels. Note how the emission morphology changes between channels and identify which channels contain significant line emission.

As you inspect the cube, consider the following questions:

  • In which channels is the emission visible?
  • Is the emission compact or extended?
  • Does the morphology change from channel to channel?
  • Which channels contain genuine signal?

These observations will guide the cleaning strategy.

5. Final Cleaned Cube

Having inspected the dirty cube and estimated the noise level, we can now perform a full deconvolution.

A common approach is to clean down to approximately three times the RMS noise: 

threshold = 3 × RMS

During the interactive CLEAN process we will construct masks around regions of genuine emission. These masks guide the deconvolution algorithm and prevent CLEAN components from being placed on noise peaks.

Linux

tclean(
 vis='twhya_selfcal.ms.contsub',
 imagename='twhya_final_cube',
 field='5',
 spw='0',
 specmode='cube',
 perchanweightdensity=True,
 nchan=15,
 start='0.0km/s',
 width='0.5km/s',
 outframe='LSRK',
 restfreq=restfreq,
 deconvolver='hogbom',
 gridder='standard',
 imsize=[250,250],
 cell='0.1arcsec',
 weighting='briggsbwtaper',
 robust=0.5,
 restoringbeam='common',
 interactive=True,
 niter=100000,
 threshold=threshold,
 pbcor=True)

Interactive Cleaning

During the CLEAN process, create masks around regions of genuine emission and continue cleaning until the residuals become noise-like.

Continue cleaning until the residual image appears noise-like and no significant emission remains in the residuals.

The resulting cube should provide a much more faithful representation of the underlying source structure.

Extracting Spectra in CARTA

Spectral profiles can be extracted interactively from selected regions of the cube using CARTA.

The final science product is: 

twhya_final_cube.image.pbcor

Begin by measuring the RMS noise in several line-free channels and compare the values with those obtained for the dirty cube.

Next inspect the cube in CARTA and explore the data both spatially and spectrally:

  • Step through the channels.
  • Extract spectra from different regions.
  • Identify the velocity range of emission.
  • Examine the spatial distribution as a function of velocity.

7. Moment Maps

Moment maps provide a compact way of summarising information contained within a spectral cube.

  • Moment 0: Integrated intensity map.
  • Moment 1: Intensity-weighted velocity field.
  • Moment 2: Velocity dispersion map.

Moment maps are powerful diagnostic tools but can be sensitive to noise. It is therefore important to restrict the calculation to channels containing genuine emission. 

# In CASA
immoments(
 'twhya_final_cube.image.pbcor',
 outfile='twhya_mom_trial',
 moments=[0,1,2]
)
# In CASA
immoments(
 'twhya_n2hp.image',
 outfile='twhya_n2hp.mom0',
 includepix=[60e-3,100],
 chans='4~12',
 moments=[0,1,2]
)

Inspect the resulting moment maps in CARTA.

Compare the maps generated using the full cube with those produced using the restricted channel range and threshold. How does the noise behaviour change?

8. Export to FITS

Although CASA stores images in its own native format, FITS remains the standard format used by most astronomical software.

Exporting the continuum image, spectral cube and moment maps to FITS allows the products to be:

  • Shared with collaborators.
  • Used in other software packages.
  • Included in publications.
  • Archived for future use.
# In CASA
exportfits(
 imagename='twhya_cont.image',
 fitsimage='twhya_cont.fits',
 overwrite=True,
)
# In CASA
exportfits(
 imagename='twhya_n2hp.image',
 fitsimage='twhya_n2hp.fits',
 velocity=True,
 overwrite=True,
)
